1. Molecular Architecture of the DNA Double Helix

Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) is a double-stranded helix with an antiparallel orientation. One strand runs 5′ to 3′ while the complementary strand runs 3′ to 5′.

Purines vs. Pyrimidines

  • Purines (Double Ring): Adenine (A), Guanine (G).
  • Pyrimidines (Single Ring): Thymine (T), Cytosine (C).
  • Base pairing: A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds).

Structural Metrics

  • Diameter: 2.0 nanometers (nm).
  • Helical Turn: 3.4 nm (~10.5 base pairs per turn).
  • Major & Minor Grooves: Critical for protein binding (e.g., transcription factors).

2. The Nucleosome: First Level of Packaging

Human cells contain ~2 meters of DNA per nucleus. To fit within a ~6 µm nucleus, DNA wraps around basic proteins called **histones**.

The Histone Octamer

Composed of two copies each of core histones: H2A, H2B, H3, and H4. Rich in positively charged basic amino acids (Lysine & Arginine) that bind tightly to the negatively charged phosphate backbone of DNA.

Beads-on-a-String (11 nm)

~147 base pairs of DNA wrap 1.65 left-handed superhelical turns around each octamer. Linker DNA connects adjacent beads, stabilized by linker histone H1.

3. 30 nm Chromatin Fiber & Higher Order Loops

Nucleosomes fold into a 30-nm diameter fiber through interactions between histone tails and linker histone H1.

Solenoid & Zigzag Models

The 11 nm nucleosome chain coils into a helical solenoid structure with 6 nucleosomes per turn, compressing DNA ~40-fold total.

Scaffold Looping

The 30 nm fiber forms loops (30–100 kb) anchored to a non-histone protein nuclear scaffold (MARs/SARs via Condensin & Cohesin).

4. Heterochromatin vs. Euchromatin & Metaphase Chromosomes

Chromatin condensation dynamically regulates gene expression and cell division states.

Euchromatin vs. Heterochromatin

  • Euchromatin: Less condensed, transcriptionally active, acetylated histones.
  • Heterochromatin: Highly condensed, transcriptionally silent, methylated histones.

Metaphase Chromosome (1400 nm)

Maximum condensation achieved during mitosis (~10,000-fold compaction) to ensure error-free segregation of sister chromatids.
